Forex Market Overview

Currencies are influenced by central bank policy, macro data, risk sentiment and timing across major trading sessions.

Major pairs

EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF

High-liquidity pairs with strong participation across London and New York sessions.

Minor pairs

Crosses and higher-volatility structures

Pairs like EUR/GBP, AUD/JPY and NZD/JPY require wider context and disciplined risk control.

Exotic pairs

Selective opportunities

Higher spreads and volatility demand careful analysis and sharper execution discipline.

Sessions

Trading sessions

Asian Session

Often softer liquidity, range dynamics and event-driven moves around Tokyo and regional risk sentiment.

London Session

Highest liquidity for many major FX pairs, with strong directional flow and breakout potential.

New York Session

US macro data can lead to sharp movement and volatility, especially around overlap with London.

Forex essentials

Pip

A pip is the smallest standard price movement in a currency pair, used to measure changes in value.

Lot size

Lot size defines the trade volume and therefore the value of each pip movement.

Spread

The spread is the difference between bid and ask prices and affects the cost to initiate a trade.

Leverage

Leverage amplifies position size, but also magnifies potential risk and requires proper position sizing.

Margin

Margin is the collateral required to open and maintain a leveraged position.

Stop Loss / Take Profit

Stop loss limits risk; take profit defines a target based on a planned trade structure.

Risk/Reward

Risk/reward compares potential loss versus potential gain and supports disciplined trade selection.

Position sizing

Position sizing helps manage exposure by aligning trade size with account risk and plan design.
